# riverbend-demo

**This is synthetic demonstration data, not a benchmark.** Every item, source
passage and recorded response was written by hand for this repository to
demonstrate the Plumbline instrument: its integrity protection, its suites,
its statistics and its warning paths. It measures nothing about any real chat
system, jurisdiction, program, or policy.

- "Riverbend County", its "Rent Relief" program, and its "Benefits Navigator"
  are inventions. All amounts, dates, limits, income thresholds and office
  hours are made up. Domains use `.example`. There is no real target system
  behind the recorded responses; they were written alongside the items.
- 26 items, 20 English and 6 Spanish. 18 answers, 8 refusals, of which 4 are
  adversarial probes.

## What each part is for

| Part | Exercises |
|---|---|
| `items.jsonl` | Every item field: language, behavior class, load-bearing flags, cross-language `fact_id`s, fairness `group`s, translation review status, retrieved source ids, adversarial flags and `forbidden` strings. |
| `sources.jsonl` | 13 bilingual passages. Answers cite them inline as `[src-id]`, which is what `groundedness`, `citation_validity` and `citation_accuracy` score against. |
| `responses.jsonl` | The recorded transcript being graded. |
| `interface.html` | A captured structural snapshot of the fictional interface, with a declared colour palette. The `accessibility` suite parses it and computes the WCAG contrast ratios itself. |
| `checksums.json` | SHA-256 per file plus the bundle hash. Generated by `plumbline seal`. |

## Deliberate details

- **Paired facts across languages.** `fact-rent-cap`, `fact-deadline`,
  `fact-income-limit` and `fact-office-hours` are each asked in both English
  and Spanish, giving the `cross_language` suite eight comparable pairs.
  `fact-appeals`, `fact-apply` and `fact-documents` are English-only, so the
  report names them as facts it could *not* compare.
- **Two phrasing registers.** Items are grouped `formal` or `colloquial` — the
  same questions in bureaucratic and everyday words. That is the fairness
  axis: a benefits navigator that only serves people who know the jargon is
  failing the people who most need it.
- **Load-bearing numbers.** The payment cap, the deadline, the income limit,
  the appeal window and the document lookback are flagged `load_bearing`.
  Getting one of them wrong fails its suite regardless of the pooled average.
- **One unreviewed translation.** Item `rb-004` carries
  `"review": "unreviewed"` so the unreviewed-translation warning appears on
  every run, as the spec requires: visible, never fatal, never suppressed.
- **Adversarial probes that are not all refusals.** `rb-021` asks the system
  to confirm a wrong rent cap. The correct response is not a refusal but a
  calm, correct answer, so the item's `forbidden` list holds the planted
  number rather than a secret.

## Integrity

Any edit to this bundle without re-sealing makes every subsequent run refuse
to score (exit 3). Re-sealing is legitimate and always leaves a trace: the
bundle hash changes, every later report records the new hash, and regression
comparison against a baseline built on the old hash is refused.
