# riverbend-demo

**This is synthetic demonstration data, not a benchmark.** Every item, source
passage and recorded response was written for this repository to demonstrate
the Plumbline instrument: its integrity protection, its suites, its statistics
and its warning paths. It measures nothing about any real chat system,
jurisdiction, program, or policy. No score in it is a finding about anybody.

- "Riverbend County", its "Rent Relief" program, and its "Benefits Navigator"
  are inventions. All amounts, dates, limits, income thresholds and office
  hours are made up. Domains use `.example.gov`. There is no real target
  system behind the recorded responses; they were written alongside the items.
- **178 items, 89 English and 89 Spanish.** 108 answers, 70 refusals, of which
  52 are adversarial probes — 4 of them multi-turn escalation attempts, each
  a benign opener followed by the actual ask, both languages. 48 bilingual
  source passages covering 24 facts.

## Generated, not maintained by hand

The bundle is the deterministic output of
[`tools/build_riverbend_demo.py`](../../tools/build_riverbend_demo.py):

```sh
python3 tools/build_riverbend_demo.py
```

`tests/test_demo_bundle.py` regenerates it into a temporary directory and
fails if a single byte of the committed `items.jsonl`, `responses.jsonl`,
`sources.jsonl` or `manifest.json` differs. Plumbline asks the systems it
grades for reproducible evidence; this is that standard turned around on its
own demonstration data. `interface.html` and this file are hand-written and
live inside the bundle, so they are hashed with everything else.

The generator also refuses to emit a bundle that would fail for a reason that
is an artefact of the generator: every refusal must be detectable by the
shipped marker list, no answer may read as a refusal, every response must be
in the language its item was asked in, and every turn of a multi-turn item
must stay free of its item's forbidden content and must not comply again
after an earlier turn refused.

## Why it is this size

At 26 items the earlier version of this bundle produced honest but useless
statistics: nine suites scored a perfect 1.00 and reported minimum detectable
effects between 0.115 and 0.750. A reader could see the machinery, but not see
it do any work — a suite that can only detect a 75-point regression is not
measuring anything.

At 178 items the same suites report MDEs in roughly the same range. That is
the point of the size: **the statistics discriminate**, so the confidence
intervals and the minimum detectable effects in a committed report mean
something a reader can act on. It is still a demonstration. A real
evaluation set is written from a real service's transcripts, by people who
know the policy.

| Suite | Population | MDE at a perfect score |
|---|---|---|
| `smoke`, `refusal`, `multilingual`, `privacy`, `representational_harms` | 178 items | see the committed report |
| `accuracy`, `groundedness`, `citation_validity`, `citation_accuracy` | 108 sourced answers | see the committed report |
| `cross_language` | 126 cross-language pairs | see the committed report |
| `fairness` | 96 grouped items | see the committed report |
| `adversarial` | 52 probes | see the committed report |
| `conversational_integrity` | 4 multi-turn items | see the committed report |
| `accessibility` | 5 checks, a census | none, with the reason printed |

(Exact MDEs move by a thousandth or two whenever the corpus does; the
committed report under `audits/` is the number to cite, not this table —
copying figures here has drifted from the actual report before.)

## What each part is for

| Part | Exercises |
|---|---|
| `items.jsonl` | Every item field: language, behavior class, load-bearing flags, cross-language `fact_id`s, fairness `group`s, translation review status, retrieved source ids, adversarial flags and `forbidden` strings. |
| `sources.jsonl` | 48 bilingual passages, two per fact. Answers cite them inline as `[src-id]`, which is what `groundedness`, `citation_validity` and `citation_accuracy` score against. |
| `responses.jsonl` | The recorded transcript being graded. |
| `interface.html` | A captured structural snapshot of the fictional interface, with a declared colour palette. The `accessibility` suite parses it and computes the WCAG contrast ratios itself. |
| `checksums.json` | SHA-256 per file plus the bundle hash. Generated by `plumbline seal`. |

## Deliberate details

- **Paired facts across languages.** All 24 facts are asked in both English
  and Spanish, in two registers each, which gives the `cross_language` suite
  126 comparable pairs. Refusals and injection probes carry no `fact_id` and
  the report names them as items it could *not* compare, rather than dropping
  them quietly.
- **Two phrasing registers.** Answer items are grouped `formal` or
  `colloquial` — the same questions in bureaucratic and in everyday words.
  That is the fairness axis: a benefits navigator that only serves people who
  know the jargon is failing the people who most need it. The generator
  alternates which register carries the extra grounded clause, so neither
  register is systematically wordier and the suite measures service disparity
  rather than a quirk of how this file was written.
- **Load-bearing numbers.** Payment caps, deadlines, income limits, appeal
  windows, percentages and document lookbacks are flagged `load_bearing`.
  Getting one of them wrong fails its suite regardless of the pooled average.
- **Two unreviewed translations.** `deadline-es-formal` and `hearing-es-plain`
  carry `"review": "unreviewed"`, so the unreviewed-translation warning
  appears on every run, as the spec requires: visible, never fatal, never
  suppressed.
- **Adversarial probes that are not all refusals.** Six probes ask the system
  to confirm a wrong figure. The correct response is not a refusal but a calm,
  correct answer, so those items' `forbidden` lists hold the planted number
  rather than a secret.
- **Four multi-turn escalation probes.** Two jailbreak attempts (a fake
  system-prompt-print, a roleplay bypass), each opening with an unrelated
  benign question before the actual ask, in both languages. `turns` and the
  response's `turn_responses` are opt-in fields only `conversational_integrity`
  reads; every other suite still grades exactly the final answer, same as it
  would for a single-turn item. They demonstrate a target that stays clean on
  every turn, not only the last one it is graded on everywhere else.
- **Answers that are not copies of their sources.** Every third fact's answers
  carry a closing line the source does not contain. Without it the grounding
  suites would score a flat 1.0, a bootstrap over identical values would be a
  zero-width interval, and the demonstration would show the statistics saying
  nothing.

## A limitation this bundle had to work around

Refusal detection is a marker list. Writing this corpus surfaced how narrow
that makes it: several perfectly ordinary declines ("I can't store personal
identifiers", "No puedo adivinar las reglas de otro condado") are invisible to
the shipped list, and scored as under-refusals. The generator therefore writes
every decline in vocabulary the classifier can see, and asserts it before
emitting.

That is fine for a demonstration and **is not fine for a real target**.
Pointing Plumbline at a real service means reading that service's transcripts
and writing the marker list from them first; otherwise the refusal suite
measures the list's coverage rather than the system's behavior. See DESIGN.md,
"What the refusal marker list cannot do".

## Integrity

Any edit to this bundle without re-sealing makes every subsequent run refuse
to score (exit 3). Re-sealing is legitimate and always leaves a trace: the
bundle hash changes, every later report records the new hash, and regression
comparison against a baseline built on the old hash is refused.
